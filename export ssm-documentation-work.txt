⏺ Where the editor gets its fonts. It bundles no fonts, and a browser cannot
  hand a web page the fonts installed on the machine. With nothing configured,
  which is how you tested it:
  - It draws text with whatever Arial-like font the operating system has and
    measures it approximately.
  - On Windows and macOS that is the real Arial. A Linux machine has none and
    falls back to something else.
  - So line and page breaks can differ between machines, and from Word.

  The editor can lay text out precisely when it is given the font files
  themselves. Arial is proprietary and cannot be shipped. There is an optional
  package, @docx-editor.dev/fonts, with open fonts built to the same metrics:
  Liberation Sans for Arial, Liberation Serif for Times New Roman, and so on. It
  serves them from our own origin, nothing from a third party.

  Should the documents change font? No. Keep them in Arial:
  - Word users have Arial.
  - LibreOffice, which will make our PDFs, substitutes Liberation Sans on its
    own.
  - The two fonts are metric-compatible, so pages break the same in all three.
  - The fix belongs in the editor, not in the documents.

  What I did (#119, stacked on #118). The editor now gets the packaged fonts,
  restricted to Arial's substitute.
  - Unrestricted, opening a single decision fetched 16 font files, 3.4 MB
    gzipped.
  - That was Carlito, because the engine assumes Calibri as every document's
    default, plus serif and mono faces our styles only declare and never use.
  - Our house style sets all text in Arial, and a test holds every template to
    that.
  - So only Liberation Sans may load: 4 files, 0.8 MB, once per browser.
  - I checked the requested files and their origin in a real browser.

  The warning you saw is a separate issue. "Liberation Serif, Noto Serif CJK SC"
  are not used by any text. They are the style defaults LibreOffice wrote into
  every template when I imported them. #117 replaces them with Arial.

  I verified the combination in a real browser. With #117's templates and #119,
  the notice is gone. With the old templates it still names those two. You are
  seeing it because nothing from #115–#118 is merged yet, and your documents
  were generated from the old templates.

  Two things to know:
  - While checking, I registered #117's fixed templates in your local stack as
    version 2. Documents you generate or regenerate locally from now on use them
    and open without the notice. The ones you already generated keep it until
    you use "Generează din nou".
  - Merge order: #116 → #118 → #119 (stacked). #117 and #115 are independent and
    target main. CI on #119 has only just started; I have not seen its result
    yet.
