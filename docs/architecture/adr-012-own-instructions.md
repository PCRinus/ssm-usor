# ADR 012: The own instructions, a common part with the organization's instruction modules as annexes

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-09-25

## Context

[ADR 011](adr-011-protective-equipment.md) built the first of the three documents that follow the client's posts and left the own instructions (3.2, _instrucțiuni proprii de securitate și sănătate în muncă_, IPSSM) and the training themes (4.2) for next. It also said the two read one more fact on the job position, which instruction modules apply to it, and left how the library of modules is kept to this decision.

The own instructions are the longest document of the set, some seventy pages. The provider's two sample packs show the same shape. Chapters I to XII are a common part: why the instructions exist, how workers are trained, conduct, traffic, electrical equipment, first aid, fire, hygiene, vulnerable groups, manual handling, protective equipment in general. It is the same text in both packs apart from the client's name, a table of the posts with their periodic training interval, and one addition on wearing each category of protective equipment. Chapter XIII holds the client-specific instructions, one self-contained block per activity or piece of equipment, each with its own articles: for the gelato bar, food service with its dishwasher, freezers and espresso machine, offices, and cleaning; for the installation company, company cars, offices, hand and electric tools, an angle grinder, welding, ladders, work at height and cryogenic installations. The offices and ladders blocks are word for word the same in both files. Articles are automatic list numbering; the common part runs from 1 to 294, and in the gelato bar's file each block restarts at 1. The training themes cite these numbers per post, which is why 4.2 follows 3.2.

The trade works with the same unit. Providers sell own instructions one per trade, activity or piece of equipment, hundreds of them, delivered as separate files with the buyer's name on them, and advise buying one per activity a worker performs; sector bundles exist for retail, food service, construction, offices. The Labour Inspection's 2018 brochure on own instructions describes an instruction as written for an activity, post or workplace, approved by the employer, and lists what one should contain at least: its scope, the qualification required, the correct way of working, the operations that carry risk, the risks with their measures including protective equipment and signage, the forbidden actions, what to do in an emergency, and the acts it draws on. It says nothing about binding them. The provider's chapter XIII blocks are such instructions, and chapters I to XII are the bundle's shared preamble. The commercial catalogues are not a source of text: they are sold personalised, some under Labour Inspection approval, and copying them is out.

A competitor's user guide (ssm.ro) shows the same unit in software. Its "Instrucțiuni SSM" section is a library of files the user uploads, one instruction each, in Word, PDF or video, which are signed and approved and carry a question bank each. Its posts group occupations that share the same risks, and the training themes for the workplace, periodic and supplementary training are generated per post by picking instructions from that library, with a tab per period of the year; the individual training records and the annual tests then follow from the approved themes. It ships no instruction text of its own, and it never binds the instructions into one document: each stays a file, and the themes refer to them by title. Its tenant is the employer, so an instruction is approved once and belongs to that company; ours is a provider serving many clients, which is the one place this decision has to differ.

Two designs were considered and rejected before being built. The first was a built-in catalogue: every block from the two packs as a conditional section inside one template, and each position ticking the ones that apply. Twenty blocks from a gelato bar and an installation company do not cover the third client, and the provider holds the block for a bakery or a warehouse in their own files; with the catalogue frozen in the template, their only way to add it would be typing it into a generated document, where it is lost at the next regeneration and never reused, which is the copy-paste the product exists to end. The second was an assembled document: modules as the organization's Word files, copied into the common part at generation, renumbered and re-styled on the way. That keeps the provider's bound format, and it was dropped because every provider's files come from a different Word, with different styles, list definitions and anchored pictures, and a merge that handles the two packs at hand would be patched per new customer for as long as the product lives. Restricting the editor to a subset, or replacing it with a structured one of our own, only narrows that: the file stays Word, and the merge stays.

## Decision

### An instruction module is the organization's file, kept as it is

An **instruction module** (_instrucțiune specifică_) is one self-contained own instruction for a work activity, a piece of work equipment, or a category of protective equipment: a title, a group, and a Word file. The file is the module, as the file is the document in [ADR 005](adr-005-document-generation.md): the app never merges it, never rewrites it, and never puts anything inside it. Modules belong to the organization and live in its **instruction library** (_biblioteca de instrucțiuni_), an entry of its own in the sidebar, "Instrucțiuni", beside the clients. It is not a section of the organization page: those sections are settings, entered once, while the library is content the specialist works in day to day. A module is kept in versions identified by the hash of the file, like a template version, and never changes in place: saving stores a new version, and every generated document records which version it annexed.

The library starts empty. The app ships no instruction text: every provider in this trade already holds their instructions, one file per activity, since that is how they are written and sold, and those files are the library. Seeding the blocks of the two sample packs was considered and dropped, as only offices, ladders and company cars are general and the rest are one client's kitchen or one client's site; seeding them for the one provider whose files they are was dropped too, as operational overhead for a library they upload themselves. A starter set of general modules can come later, once a second provider says which ones they want. The dev seed and the flow tests use two or three small modules kept in the repository as fixtures.

There are two ways into the library. **Uploading Word files**, several at once, makes one module per file: the upload dialog proposes each file's first heading as the title, the provider corrects it and sets the group, and the file is stored as uploaded. This is the import path for a provider's existing library and the mechanism uploaded documents already use, `.docx` only, never `.doc`. **Writing one in the app** starts from a skeleton the app ships in the house style, with the brochure's headings and one numbered article. Either way the provider changes a module afterwards in the same editor documents open in, with its full toolbar, or replaces its file by uploading again. The one transformation on upload is the font sweep the templates already receive, so the editor does not show its fonts notice; it touches the file's style table and nothing else, and it can be skipped for a file it cannot read.

Re-styling an uploaded file into the house style, whether by a parser of our own or by a language model on a Cloudflare AI Worker, is not part of this decision. The unbundled document does not need it: a module prints as the provider wrote it, and the PDF converter renders any Word file. It stays a possible later addition, best-effort and reviewed by the provider in the editor, with two guards if it is ever built: the wording is copied verbatim and verified against the source, since instruction text is legal content and no model may rewrite it, and the source file is kept, so a poor result costs one provider a retry rather than anyone a broken document. Where a model would earn its place is structure, not style: splitting a bound instructions document into modules by its headings, proposing a title and a group, and turning numbering typed as text into real lists.

A module is archived, never deleted, and archiving is refused while any active position of any client applies it; the message names them. Titles are unique within the organization, ignoring case. Owners and specialists both edit modules, as they both edit documents.

### Every position applies modules, or none

A job position is in one of three states about instructions, as it is about equipment: **undecided**, **needs none** beyond the common part, or it **applies** a list of modules. New positions are undecided. Generating the own instructions requires every current position to have decided, and the generation form names the undecided ones with a link to the position page. A position can copy the modules of another position of the same client.

Choosing modules at the client level was rejected. The training themes have to say which instructions concern each post, and chapter I of the document itself promises that delimitation per post. A client's document annexes the union of the modules its current positions apply, each once, in the order of the library's groups and then by title. A post applies whole modules; a provider who needs part of one for a post splits the module. Archived positions are ignored, as ADR 006 says.

### What the document is

The own instructions are the **common part**, generated from a built-in template like any document, with the usual fixes and these departures from the provider's file:

- The table of contents lists the chapters without page numbers, and under chapter XIII the annexed modules by title. The provider's typed page ranges cannot survive a list that changes per client, and neither the editor nor the PDF step updates a Word table of contents field.
- The table of posts and intervals in chapter II prints one row per current position: its work zone, its name, its interval, the position's own where it has one and the client's category interval otherwise, and the client's periodic duration.
- Chapter XIII prints the list of annexes: "Anexa n: I.P.S.S.M. Title", with the version's date, one per applied module. The employer's approval of the document, on its first page, covers the annexes by name. When no position applies a module, the chapter says so in one line. The protective equipment modules are listed with the others rather than under chapter XII, where the installation company's file has them: chapter XII's opening sentence, that the instructions cover each item of equipment in use, is satisfied either way.

The Word file of the document is the common part alone. The modules stay the library's files, and the training themes, when built, cite a module by its title, and by "Art. 1–N" where N is the count of the file's top-level numbered items, a harmless guess since it is only a citation. The common part's own article numbers are fixed by the template, so every citation of chapters I to XII in the themes is static text, held to the template's chapter counts by a test.

### Issuing makes one PDF

Issuing converts the common part and each annexed module, in order, and merges them into the one PDF the revision stores and the client receives as its binder. The converter's document route takes several files and merges them in one request, so this is the existing PDF step with more than one file, not a new mechanism. Page numbers run per part: the common part is numbered by its template, and a module's file is numbered as its author left it. The issued Word download is the common part; a module's Word file is downloaded from the library.

Because the client's document never contains module text, nothing can flow back from a document to the library, and a client-specific wording of a module is a module of its own. This is the discipline the library wants: a correction made for one client is either the module's, and then every client gets it, or it is a new module.

### The snapshot holds module versions

The data snapshot a revision is generated from records, per position, the modules applied and the version of each. Editing a module in the library therefore shows "Date modificate" on every draft and issued revision of every client whose document annexed the old version, and regenerating annexes the current one; an issued revision stays locked and gets a new draft beside it. The training themes read the module versions of the client's latest own instructions revision rather than the library's current versions, so that what they cite is what the client's document annexes, and their readiness requires a generated own instructions document.

### In the app

- "Instrucțiuni" in the sidebar, for owners and specialists alike: the library as a list by group with each module's state and where it is applied, a page per module with the editor, the upload of one or several files, and archiving, and a button for a new module from the skeleton. Later libraries of reusable content, such as the risk sheets of stage 3, would be siblings of this entry, not tabs of it.
- The job position page gains a card "Instrucțiuni specifice" beside the equipment card: the applied modules by group, the needs-none choice, the copy from another position, and a picker over the library that opens a module read-only in the editor for a look. The positions table gains a column with the state, "4 instrucțiuni", "nu necesită" or "nedecis".
- The own instructions leave the uploaded document types and join the generated set, as the equipment list did; a document uploaded before this change stays as it is until regenerated.

## Consequences

- The document engine does not change. No merge of foreign Word files, no validation gate, no restriction of the editor. The PDF step gains a multi-file conversion, and the issued PDF of this document is the first that is not the conversion of its own Word file.
- The provider's bound format is given up: the Word file of the own instructions is the common part, and the binder exists as the issued PDF. A provider who wants one Word file has the modules' files beside it.
- The organization owns content that documents read, for the first time. The library is the provider's asset; a provider who switches organizations loses it, and export is a later question.
- A module prints as its author formatted it, so a binder can change font and layout between the common part and an annex. The font sweep on upload is the only concession, and the house style is the provider's to apply in the editor if they want it.
- The per-position burden before the first generation grows again: equipment, and now instructions, for every post. The generation form has to keep naming exactly what is missing.
- A new organization cannot generate the own instructions until its library holds what its clients' positions need. Uploading the files they already have is the first step of onboarding for this document.
- The training themes are tied to a revision of the own instructions, not to the library, and cite modules by title and a counted range rather than by numbers inside one bound document.
- The only module files in the repository are the fixtures of the dev seed and the tests.
